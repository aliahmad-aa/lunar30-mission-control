📡 SOURCES & SCIENCE

🔵 1 — Actual NASA Observations

  • Water Ice at Lunar South Pole
    Confirmed by NASA's LCROSS impactor (2009): the Centaur rocket strike in Cabeus crater ejected a water-ice plume, detected by the LCROSS shepherding spacecraft. Mini-RF radar aboard LRO confirmed additional ice deposits.
    NASA LCROSS Mission →
  • Apollo Moonquake Records
    Apollo seismometers (1969–1977) recorded over 10,000 moonquakes. Shallow moonquakes can exceed magnitude 5.5 and persist for over 10 minutes — the rigid, dry interior has minimal damping. No confirmed active lunar volcanism in historic times.
    NASA Moonquakes →
  • DONKI Space-Weather Events
    The Mission Intelligence panel attempts to load actual recent solar flare (FLR) and solar energetic particle (SEP) records from the NASA DONKI API in real time. If online, records are labelled ACTUAL NASA DATA. Offline play uses SIMULATED SCENARIO labels.
    NASA DONKI Portal → | NASA Open APIs →

🟣 2 — NASA Engineering Reference Values (BVAD Table 3-31)

Source: NASA/TP-2015-218570 Rev. 2 (February 2022), "Life Support Baseline Values and Assumptions Document", NTRS ID: 20210024855, specifically Table 3-31 (Exploration Life Support Baseline Mass Balance). These represent authentic NASA engineering planning parameters:

  • Oxygen consumed: 0.895 kg / crewmember-day — based on standard metabolic workload.
  • Potable water content: 3.217 kg / crewmember-day — dedicated clean water supplied for crew direct drinking and meal preparation.
  • Water in food (before rehydration): 0.760 kg / crewmember-day — inherent moisture already contained in the pre-packaged food matrix.
  • Food solids (without packaging): 0.800 kg / crewmember-day — actual anhydrous nutrient mass (excluding packaging and inherent moisture).
  • Carbon dioxide load: 1.085 kg / crewmember-day — metabolic byproduct requiring continuous removal by ECLSS CDRA/scrubbers.

⚠️ Scientific Distinction: "Potable water" (3.217 kg) and "Water in food" (0.760 kg) are distinct mass streams in ECLSS engineering. Potable water must be dispensed from life-support tanks, whereas moisture in food is consumed directly with rations. In this simulator, resource meters represent total available reserves for accessible educational gameplay.

🟡 3 — Simplified Gameplay Parameters

The following are intentional game-design simplifications. They are not NASA data.

  • All five resources are shown as percentage of total supply, not physical units (kg, Wh, Pa).
  • The "Water" bar represents total water budget (drinking + food rehydration), not just drinking water.
  • The "Food" bar represents dry-mass food store; rehydration water is not separately tracked.
  • Crew health is abstracted to four states: Healthy / Fatigued / Critical / Incapacitated.
  • Power generation ignores the real 14-day lunar day/night cycle (simplified to 7 game days).
  • Water recycling efficiency is fixed; real ECLSS CHX and WRS efficiency varies with load and maintenance.
  • Radiation doses are advisory only. No cumulative dose model (Gy or Sv) is implemented.
  • RTG power output is instantaneous in the game; real RTGs degrade slowly over years.

🟠 4 — Fictional Mission Events

The following scenario elements are entirely fictional and do not represent real NASA mission plans or data:

  • Artemis Outpost Shackleton, crew roster, and mission designation ART-SHK-2026.
  • The specific Day 1–7 event sequence and decision trees.
  • Exact resource impacts of each player choice (tuned for gameplay balance).
